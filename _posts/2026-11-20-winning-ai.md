title: What does winning mean?
---

Anthropic became the biggest IPO on my birthday, the Riemann Hypothesis was [solved(?)](https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis/) yesterday, and there still seems to be no clear idea or road map for how this will all benefit humans. At this point, it is [foolish](https://pluralistic.net/2026/04/16/pascals-wager/#doomer-challenge) to believe that machines will not surpass human intelligence. Even if you believe intelligence derived from silicon will follow an S-curve progression, and it will plateau eventually due to [scaling laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amdahl%27s_law), it's probably a good idea to assume it plateaus at a level above human intelligence.

This computing power will either be owned and decentralized to the broader population, or it will be consolidated within the hands of a few megacorporations. So far, the latter is the state the US continues to propel itself towards, and I don't think most people want to live in that future. It's clear that frontier labs like OpenAI or Anthropic are doing their best to [fear monger](https://openai.com/index/hugging-face-incident-and-the-road-ahead/) and [lobby](https://village.dev/blog/openai-and-anthropic-doubled-their-lobbying-big-tech-stayed-flat/) their way into controlling all of the value that can come from this new intelligence. I don't have a problem with these companies wanting to monetize the models they spent billions of dollars training. The problem is when they attempt to lock down and restrict competition from evolving and allowing the market to decide the future it wants to be in. These frontier labs know there exists a future where open-weight models will be good enough, or at least  intelligent and efficient enough, to not rely on a revokable API token whose cost is artifically priced. Instead, people could have the __option__ to self-host models, manage their own inference stack, and keep their data to themselves. I prefer the direction Jensen Huang and China point towards. You can argue that Jensen's stance comes from the fact that Nvidia is selling the chips that all AI infernce requires, so his position is purely driven by financial incentives. You can also argue that China only holds this position out of necessity, because they lack the computing power and capital the US has. Outside of capital or finanicial restraints, I believe China sees AI and technology differently than the US on a [cultural level](https://asiatimes.com/2026/02/china-building-a-different-ai-future-than-the-west/), but I digress. 

Nvidia's moat is with CUDA, and selling their chips to as many countries or people as possible helps retain a software dependency on their chips, but AI is going to become intelligent enough to allow developers to translate between different hardware architectures.

These frontier AI labs have no moat. They can restrict hardware exports to China as much as they want, but unless they solve recursive self improvement with their models, open-weight models will not lag too far behind. China is squeezing so much with much less capital than the US. They are also the ones who re-engineered and introduced [mixture of experts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06066) to open-weight models, making them dramatically cheaper and smarter. If there's anyone I'm betting on to make breakthrough innovations in this space, it's the team whose [AI labs are all working together](https://aiproem.substack.com/p/part-1-deepseeks-v4-makes-chinese) in some sense like a joint AI lab, not just throwing billions of dollars torwards scaling infrastructure and making GPUs go more brrrr...Furthermore, AI is built on power output, and China is [FAR outpacing](https://www.vaneck.com/us/en/blogs/natural-resources/the-power-divide-china-us-and-the-future-of-the-grid/) the US in electricity generation. This isn't a battle the US will win, and frankly, I hope it doesn't, because a US win is only a win for a few at the top.

One day, I want an RTX 9090 with 64 GB of RAM running in my house, using an open-weight model downloaded from the internet. It'll be connected to the rest of my house as my personal assistant. The value extracted from the electricity my house uses is MINE, not [Microsoft's.](https://www.pcmag.com/news/microsoft-patent-describes-using-your-idle-gaming-pc-to-train-ai). The data it collects from me is mine. I actually own the most valuable asset right now.
